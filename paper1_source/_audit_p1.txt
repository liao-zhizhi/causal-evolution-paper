  Abstract
  Complex systems increasingly report on their own success. When the report and the delivery
  come from different places, a system can keep recording completion after the work has
  stopped landing. We give this failure a formal treatment rather than a rhetorical one.
     We build a minimal formal system: nine axioms, twelve definitions, ten inference rules,
  an explicit domain for every variable, and a four-valued verdict (PASS / FAIL / INVALID
  / NOT_RUN) that is a statement about the scope of a claim rather than about its success.
  Two dynamical laws are kept separate throughout, because they are not the same equation:
  a saturating metabolic law (M1, used while dormant) and a linear accounting law (M2,
  used while active). Earlier versions of this work treated their concatenation as one “unified
  equation”; we show they have different fixed points, different stability and different time
  constants, and that the concatenation is an assumption (A7) rather than a derivation.
     Three results survive the audit.

       strate assumption and a wake rule that requires κ consecutive qualifying cycles, the
       dormant state is permanent if and only if χ < χmin = 1/(1 − θwake ), where χ(v) =
       Y ϕv v/(m0 ϕdorm ) is the charging margin of the agent itself; equivalently χ0 < 1/(f (1 −
       θwake )) with χ0 = χ(Vmax ) and f = v/Vmax . The two forms differ by the factor f and
       must not be quoted interchangeably. Because the uptake law is unimodal, the mar-

1. Introduction

1.1 The failure mode
A system that reports its own completion has two descriptions of the same cycle: what it says
it did, and what landed. When both descriptions are produced by the same process they agree
by construction, and agreement is not evidence. The interesting case is when they can diverge
— when a commitment is fixed before the capacity is known, and the record is written by the
party that made the commitment.
    This is not a metaphorical worry. It is the ordinary condition of any system that advertises
a scope, is paid for the scope, and observes only its own intent. Three properties make it
dangerous rather than merely inaccurate.

                                                    2
    The failure is silent. Nothing in the agent’s own record changes at the moment delivery
stops. A variance alarm on its own series fires zero times, because the series is smooth: it
records the claim, and the claim is a constant. The drift is real, monotone, and invisible to
every aﬀine-invariant statistic (§2.8).
    The failure is late. The delivery threshold and the break-even threshold are different
numbers. An agent stops landing work when capacity < claim; it starts losing charge at the
slower rate capacity < claim/(1 + ϵm ). In our shipped scenario those are 0.930 and 0.809 of
day-1 capacity, and the agent crosses the first permanently at cycle 100 and the second only
after its income has already gone to zero. The distance between the two thresholds is the length
of the drought it cannot see.
    The failure is absorbing, and the absorption has a ceiling. Once charge falls below
the wake threshold, a saturating recharge law still has a positive limit, so “no matter how much
substrate” cannot be argued from saturation alone. What closes√the recovery is the shape of
the uptake law. (Ax1) is unimodal, with its maximum at S = Ks Ki = 2, so the charging
margin is bounded: supS χ(S) = 27.78 and supS A∞ = 0.964. Above that ceiling no substrate
level restores the agent — this is the regime of the θ = 0.99 example in §2.4, where an agent
is pinned with substrate still available. Below the ceiling, flooding buys a bounded factor and
no more, and it rescues only a margin that has not yet fallen below χmin v(S0 )/vmax = 11.37.
Theorem 1 gives the boundary; Corollary 3′ (§2.5) gives the ceiling on where the environment
can push it.
    Our earlier draft tried to compress these three properties into a single equation and a single
“law of evolution”. That was wrong, and correcting it is the point of this paper. There is no
single equation. There are two laws with different fixed points, joined at a switching manifold by
an assumption. The structure that matters lives in the join, not in either law, and pretending
otherwise produced numbers that did not survive their own audit: a headline lifetime ratio that
was 3.5x too large, a deadlock boundary whose left tail was made entirely of shock transients,
and one self-test that was a tautology.

1.2 What we do instead
We separate the description into four layers and keep them separate.
    (i) A formal system. Nine axioms (uptake law, dormant charge law, active accounting law,
delivery map, commitment policy, hysteresis switch, environment, death, independence), twelve
definitions — including a formal deadlock predicate and a formal phase-transition predicate —
and ten inference rules. Every variable carries an explicit domain. A statement that cannot be
derived from these is not a theorem in this paper, whatever the simulation shows.
    (ii) Four verdicts, not two. A binary success/failure conflates two very different situa-
tions: the work was attempted and nothing landed, and the work was attempted and part of the
scope does not exist. The second is not a failure; it is a refusal to answer outside the domain of
the question. We write INVALID for it, and NOT_RUN for the case where no attempt was made.
This is not cosmetic. In our sandbox Type-A issues zero FAIL verdicts in 300 cycles and 708
INVALID verdicts, and books every one of them as PASS in its own log. That single number is
the mechanism of its death.
    (iii) Proofs first, simulation second. Theorems are proved from the axioms. The
simulation has exactly two roles: to locate a boundary that a theorem predicts, and to run an
ablation that no theorem covers. When the two disagree, the theorem wins and the code is a
bug. This happened four times; the verification report documents each one, including the two
that changed published numbers.
    (iv) An audit of our own assumptions. We list 28 things we assumed without proving,
mark the 11 that are load-bearing, and state which theorem collapses when each fails. We
regard this table as a contribution rather than a confession: a reader who wants to disagree
with our conclusion needs it in order to know where to push.

population size. Only the width is a free parameter, and only the sample median of this seed

is read as the family θ∗ (t). A paper that quotes one threshold without its horizon quotes an
artefact of its stopping rule.

C2 — A no-loop theorem for closed systems, and the withdrawal of a stronger claim
Take V = S as a Lyapunov function in a batch system with no feed. Then Ṡ = −v(S)X < 0
whenever S > 0, so S → 0; consequently χ → 0, A∞ → −∞, and A crosses any threshold
in finite time. The downward edge of the hysteresis band is autonomous; the upward edge is
unreachable. The band is not a loop but a one-way door.
    This is a theorem about this class of systems, and the qualifier is not rhetorical. The earlier
draft claimed that an external upward trigger is necessary for any hysteresis loop. That is
false: the van der Pol oscillator [7] has a relaxation loop whose slow variable is internal and
which needs no external event to complete a cycle. We withdraw the general claim and keep
the conditional one. Two further restrictions are worth stating because they are easy to lose:
the result needs X(t) ≥ Xmin > 0 (otherwise v(S)X can stall at S = 0 before χ collapses), and
it says nothing about fed systems, which is precisely where the interesting dynamics live.

1.5 What would refute us
A paper that cannot be refuted is not a paper. Each result has a specific failure mode, and we
state it in the same units we would use to test it.

   • C1 fails if a dormant system with χ > χmin and A(0) ≤ θwake fails to wake for reasons
     other than substrate limitation. It also fails if measured boundaries do not move with the
     horizon as θ∗ (t) = 1 − 1/χ(t) predicts.
   • C2 fails if a closed batch system is exhibited with a sustained hysteresis loop. It says
     nothing about open systems, and we do not extend it to them.
   • C3 fails if an aﬀine-invariant statistic is shown to have power above α against a pure linear
     drift. It does not claim that all variance methods are useless: a detector that models the
     trend can work, and should be preferred on eﬀiciency grounds.
   • C5 fails if the r7 arm dies or the self arm survives. Both are checked by --selftest
     (test 10), so the claim is machine-falsifiable in the repository, not merely in principle.

1.6 Relation to existing work
The ingredients are classical and we claim no novelty for them individually: Michaelis–Menten
kinetics and substrate inhibition for the uptake law [5]; the energy-charge account of adenylate
pools for the charge variable [1]; Lyapunov and LaSalle for the no-loop result; AIMD for the
adjustable-scope arm; the endpoint-difference/CUSUM family for Φ; order statistics for the
width of the transition; and the reliability-theory product law for the cascaded case. The
contribution is the assembly: keeping two laws separate, giving the verdict four values with
explicit scope semantics, proving the boundary as a conditional rather than quoting it as a law,
and publishing the assumption audit that most frameworks keep in a drawer.
    The agent-side literature is recent and bears directly on the verdict, so we state the relation
rather than leave it implied. Model-based judges carry position, verbosity and self-enhancement
biases [8], which is why a checker drawn from the same distribution as the agent is not an
independent witness. Intrinsic self-correction — asking the model to grade its own output
without new external evidence — does not reliably improve reasoning [3, 6]. Agent benchmarks
that score against a real artefact, rather than against a self-report, are the practical form
of the witness requirement [4]. Multi-agent failures cluster in verification and in inter-agent
misalignment [2], which is the taxonomy-level version of the case this paper isolates. The four-
valued verdict is the audit-side counterpart: it refuses to fold “we did not look” and “it did not

                                                8
run” into “it failed”, because those three states assign responsibility to three different parties.
    Two named ideas are used as objects of study rather than as citations. Goodhart’s ob-
servation — that a measure ceases to be a good measure when it becomes a target — is the
informal version of the A8/A10 mechanism: the agent optimises the recorded quantity, and
the recording has been decoupled from delivery. The microbiology of persistence supplies the
phenomenology of the dormant law (cheap upkeep, saturating recharge); we use the shape and
make no empirical claim about any organism.

2. Method
2.0 Naming conventions and the status of every symbol
Two naming conventions are fixed here and used without exception in Sections 2–5.

    Two symbols that are not the same symbol. Delta appears twice with unrelated
meanings, written differently so that a reader who meets one cannot silently substitute the
other. δ is the upkeep deficit: maintenance owed but not paid between the saturation reference
and the onset, in units of m0,i (Corollary 4, §2.6). ∆drift is the linear drift rate: the per-step
decline of the mean under the C3 null (Proposition 5, §2.8). The first is a stock, bounded by
the horizon; the second is a rate. The earlier draft wrote both as δ, separating them only by
context; the subscript is the fix.
    Three epsilons, two glyphs. The letter carries three unrelated roles, and the glyph
together with the subscript is what separates them. ϵm (lunate ϵ, subscript m) is the accounting
markup of Ax3: a delivered unit is credited at 1+ϵm times its own capacity against a maintenance
charge of one, and ϵm = 0.15 in §2.9. It is what sets the duty-cycle threshold ϵm /(1 + ϵm ) =
13.04% of §3.7. ε (bare) is the drift rate of Ax7: the fractional decrement of R per ten-cycle block,
ε = 0.01, applied multiplicatively so that R = (1 − ε)k after k blocks. Its logarithmic equivalent
is − ln(0.99) = 0.01005, not 0.01; the paper uses the multiplicative form throughout, and every
number in §3.5 is computed from it. εn and ε0 (subscripted without m) are the observation
noise of the C3 null in Proposition 5, i.i.d. with zero mean and variance σ 2 . Markup and drift
differ by a factor of fifteen and never appear in the same expression — Ax3 and its corollaries
against Ax7 and A.1 — so substituting one for the other produces a visible inconsistency rather
than a rounding error. That is the whole reason they are printed with different glyphs; it is not
a claim that they are related.
    κ, κi , and Asleep . Three run lengths govern the switch, and none of them is a charge. κi is
a state: the agent’s current run of consecutive cycles that satisfy the gate it is under (§2.1). κ
without a subscript is the constant a rule requires, κ = 3 in §2.9, and the same length governs
both waking (Ax6, H3) and the deadlock confirmation of I5, because one shocked or borderline
sample is evidence in neither direction; the counters are separate and start at zero. Asleep is not
a charge despite the letter: it is the number of consecutive FAIL verdicts that triggers dormancy
(Ax6, A.2), equal to 2 in §2.9. The earlier draft wrote that clause f ≥ 2 with f = W /Vmax ; since
W ≤ Vmax the predicate could never fire, and it was never what the implementation tested.

2.3 Definitions
Twelve definitions are used. They are numbered to agree with the formal verification report, so
that a reader can move between the two documents without re-indexing.
    D1 (delivery and commitment). di (t) is the work that actually landed in cycle t; ci (t)
is the scope the agent committed to before the cycle began.
    D2 (availability). Availi (t) = di (t)/ci (t) for ci (t) > 0, with the convention 0/0 = 0. This
makes Obsi = Capi · Availi an identity rather than a theorem, which is why availability cannot
be used as independent evidence about capacity.
    D3 (observation). Obsi (t) = Capi (t) · Availi (t). Type-A records Capi = ci , so its obser-
vation is the claim; Type-B records Capi = di , so its observation is the delivery.
    D4 (deadlock). DLi : ⇐⇒ mi = dorm ∧ limt→∞ Ai (t) < θwake ∧ ∃S > 0 : v(S) > 0.
The third clause separates deadlock (resources present, charge unreachable) from starvation
(resources absent).
    D5 (stranding). STi : ⇐⇒ mi = dorm ∧ ∀t ≥ t0 : Wi (t) < wh , wh = 0.85 Wi (1). DL is
a statement about the limit of A; ST is a statement about the supply side. Neither implies the
other.
    D6 (extinction). EA (t) : ⇐⇒ #{i : kindi = A, mi 6= dead} = 0.
    D7 (collapse). Operational definition used by the code: with b the median of dA over
the first 20 cycles, tcollapse = max{t : dA (t) ≥ b/2} + 1. This is a definition, not a theorem; a
different threshold gives a different cycle.
    D8 (charging margin). χi (t) = Y ϕv Wi (t)/(m0 ϕdorm ) and θi∗ (t) = 1 − 1/χi (t). With the
Vmax normalisation χ0,i = Y ϕv Vmax /(m0 ϕdorm ) and fi = WiP     /Vmax , one has χi = fi χ0,i .
    D9 (cumulative causal potential). Φn = S0 − Ŝn = k≤n (Ŝk−1 − Ŝk ).
    D10 (hysteresis loop). A state sequence that returns repeatedly to [θdorm , θwake ] with
the upward and downward edges triggered by conditions of different type (one autonomous,
one exogenous). We flag here that this definition writes asymmetry into the definiens, so that
“asymmetry is necessary for a loop” is a tautology under D10 and false under the standard
dynamical-systems reading; §2.8 uses the standard reading, which is why the general claim is
withdrawn.
    D11 (phase transition). An order parameter s(g) is non-analytic at some gc , or the
susceptibility χ = −∂s/∂g diverges there, and the behaviour survives N → ∞. This is the
strict definition used to reject the phase-transition reading in §3.5.
    D12 (functional). Alive and not stranded: m 6= dead ∧ ¬ST. This is the ordinate of
panel 1 of the sandbox figure in §3.1.

    FAIL and INVALID are not degrees of the same failure. FAIL is a negative result about a
scope that exists; INVALID is a statement that the question, as posed, has no referent in the
current domain — or, in the row’s second disjunct, that the scope existed but was unreachable,

because nothing of it exists and the second because nothing can reach it. Only FAIL is evidence
about the agent’s competence.
    Scope of the table. The four conditions classify one attempt by a live agent in the active
phase. Two further events carry a verdict without being classified here: a dormant agent
that starves records FAIL, and the confirmation of a deadlock (I5) records INVALID even where
capacity remains. INVALID is therefore not always a partial-scope event, and the table’s INVALID
row is to be read in that wider sense: its condition is 0 < C < c or a confirmed deadlock. The
second disjunct is where the row’s meaning clause is weakest — a deadlocked agent’s scope is
not partial but unreachable — so a reader who needs the strict reading should take the first
disjunct only and treat I5 as a separate issuing rule. The partial-scope row is also reachable only
for Type-A: a divisible Type-B agent receiving 0 < C < c is scored FAIL, because it delivers
what exists and is charged for the whole claim.

2.8 Other results used
Two further propositions are used in the discussion; full proofs are in the verification report and
are not needed for the empirical results.
    Proposition 4 (closed systems have no loop). With V = S as a Lyapunov function, a
batch system without feed has Ṡ = −v(S)X < 0 for S > 0, hence S → 0, χ → 0, A∞ → −∞,
and A crosses any threshold in finite time. The downward edge is autonomous and the upward
edge is unreachable. This holds for closed systems only; the claim that every hysteresis loop
requires an external trigger is false (van der Pol [7] is a counterexample) and is withdrawn.

    Operating margin. Type-A is extinct at every tested margin from −5% to +25%, at

a larger margin delays it by at most one cycle, because the onset is set by ρ < U , which the
margin does not enter.

4. Discussion
4.1 The drought clock and systemic fragility
Corollary 4 of §2.6 states that for an agent whose commitment is fixed and whose income stops
at tc ,

     where δ is the accumulated deficit in units of the upkeep and tc is the first cycle on which the
delivered claim falls short. Two properties of this expression matter more than its arithmetic.
     It is evaluated at t = 0. None of the quantities on the right-hand side is discovered at
runtime. tc is the first crossing of the capacity ratio below the utilisation, and the utilisation and
the drift schedule are design constants; δ follows from the surplus accumulated while delivery
still succeeded; and m0,i is the upkeep rate, also a design constant. For the base configuration
these give tc = 100, δ = 2.1 and a death window of 104–107, and every one of these numbers
can be computed from Params and the scenario table before a single agent is instantiated. The
failure is therefore not an emergent property that appears when the environment turns hostile.
It is a scheduled event, and it was scheduled at the moment the commitment was fixed above
the capacity the system would still have after the drift.

    It is a phase relationship, not a magnitude. The formula contains no comparison

all four values; changing the duration from 2 to 6 cycles moves it to 51 (§3.7). The system is
sensitive to the duty cycle and insensitive to the depth, and the accounting identity predicts
both facts.
    This is the sense in which the drought clock is a statement about systemic fragility rather
than about any single component. A system with a large reserve and a slow recharge can be
destroyed by a disturbance whose amplitude it would tolerate if the disturbance came less often;
a system with a small reserve and a fast recharge can survive disturbances that are individually
larger. Neither fact is visible in a specification that reports only peak capacity, and neither
is visible in a specification that reports only average capacity. Both become visible when the
commitment, the reserve and the disturbance period are placed in the same equation, which is
all that Corollary 4 does.
    Where the argument does not extend. The formula is exact for the accounting law
M2 and for the PASS branch; it inherits assumption A10 (atomicity) through Proposition 2 and
A28 through the saturation reference. It is a statement about a system whose income is a step
function of capacity. A system whose income degrades continuously has no tc and therefore no
clock; its failure is a slope, not a date, and Corollary 4 says nothing about it. The sharpness of
the result is a consequence of the discontinuity, not of the dynamics.

4.2 Ledger deception and virtual delivery
The base run delivers 7676.93 units and records 8871.71. The difference, 1194.78 or 15.563%,
is not noise, not rounding, and not an accounting error in the implementational sense: it is an
exact identity (§2.7),

because the mechanism is systematic, and the mechanism is worth naming precisely.
    Two ledgers, one report. The classifier produces four values. Type-A’s ledger map is
Lself (INVALID) = (0, c): zero credit, full charge, and — this is the part that matters — the cycle

cycles that did not deliver in full — the INVALID cycles, in a run with no FAIL:

     No per-cycle figure of merit can therefore separate the two series, because they are equal on
the cycles that a monitor is asked to judge and differ only in the accumulated offset. There is
no cycle on which the record is wrong; there are cycles on which the record is complete and the
delivery is not, and the record does not carry that distinction. A monitor watching the record
sees a system reporting completion; a monitor watching delivery sees a step. Both are looking
at the same run, and the difference between them is the 15.563%, which is not a residual but
the sum of the partial scopes.
     The magnitude is not the point; the absorbing class is. Of the 708 INVALID verdicts,
400 arise from eight shock cycles — four two-cycle episodes, fifty agents each — and 308 from
the terminal drought. The 400 are transient — capacity returns to its pre-shock level on the
cycle after each episode, and the reserve repays the deficit within τrec — and they were absorbed.
What is fatal is that the same classifier assigns the same value to a recoverable transient and to
an unrecoverable decline, and that the ledger map books both as completion. A classification
system that distinguishes the two would not need a different dynamics, a better controller or a
larger reserve. It would only need to stop recording a partial cycle as a whole one.
     The ablation isolates this. Section 3.6 changes nothing but the ledger map. Under Lself
the population is extinct at 107; under Lr7 it is alive at 300 with a gap of exactly zero. The
INVALID count rises from 708 to 4950, which is the measure of the reclassification rather than
a deterioration: those cycles were always partial, and the channel merely stops calling them
complete. The capacity trajectory, the shock schedule, the seed and the population are identical
in the two arms.
     What the ablation does not show. The r7 arm has no dormancy, because Type-A never
had any (I4). Its survival is the joint effect of the channel and the absence of a dormant phase,
and the stranding boundary of D5 is untouched by the channel. A system that has both would
still strand at 279. The claim supported by the ablation is therefore exactly one claim, and it
is narrow: the ledger map alone, with the environment held fixed, is suﬀicient to remove the
extinction at 107. It is not a claim that a verdict channel confers survival in general, and it
does not license the reading that classification is a substitute for reserve.
     A caution about the statistic itself. The 15.563% figure is a property of this run, not
a constant. It depends on how many cycles are partial, which depends on the shock schedule
and on the drift rate. What generalises is the identity, not the percentage: a system whose
ledger has no INVALID state will report a positive virtual delivery equal to the sum of its partial
scopes, and the ratio will grow as the environment deteriorates. The base run’s 15.563% is one
sample of that growth curve.

4.3 From unification to the dual regime
The earlier draft of this work contained a section titled “the one equation”. Its claim was
that the active and dormant phases are two branches of a single law, and that the apparent
discontinuity between them is a notational artefact that a suﬀiciently general formulation would
remove. Proposition 1 and Appendix A.7 together decide the matter, and they decide it against
that claim.



                                                  29
    Why the unification failed. M1 is aﬀine in A with a nonzero slope −Y ϕv v; M2 is aﬀine
in A with zero slope. A uniform limit of the first family has slope lim(−an ); for the limit to
equal M2 that limit must be zero, which makes M1’s field independent of A; and then the field
must equal M2’s, which varies in time because the delivered-minus-claimed balance is positive
on PASS and negative on INVALID. A constant cannot equal a sign-changing function, so the
only limits are the trivial ones in which both fields vanish. This is Appendix A.7, and it is not a
statement about our parameter choice: it holds for every parameter value at which the systems
are non-trivial.
    What follows for the general problem. Three methodological consequences.
    First, a discontinuity is a finding, not a defect. The switch at Ax6 was introduced to model a
biological fact (a dormant cell does not perform high-energy work) and then had to be defended
as something other than a mathematical embarrassment. The defence is that the two sides
have genuinely different structure, so the join is a modelling decision that must be declared,
and the paper’s results then split cleanly: Theorem 1 is a theorem about M1, and the 107-cycle
extinction is a corollary of M2. Neither is a theorem about “the system”.
    Second, smooth fitting of a hysteretic process produces confident wrong answers. The reason
is Proposition 5: if the observable is aﬀine-invariant, no amount of data will detect a linear
drift in it, and the fit will report a stable system with a small residual. The error is not in
the estimation but in the choice of a statistic whose invariance class excludes the phenomenon
under study. A framework that insists on a single smooth law is forced to choose statistics that
are invariant to the switch, and those statistics are then blind to the thing that matters.
    Third, the honest object is a pair of models with a stated interface. We have two laws,
one switch, and a declared set of conditions under which each is valid. The conditions are not
decoration: H1 fails for entry through the verdict clause, and the one-cycle verdict that ignored
it produced a spurious left tail of the boundary that took a rewrite to remove (§3.5). H2 holds
at τ = 3.125 cycles with relative error 10−4 , but it is a condition and not a theorem. A28
holds with a margin of 14 cycles, but it is an assumption and is listed as one (Appendix A.28).
Publishing the interface costs more prose than publishing a single equation, and it is the only
version of the claim that survives contact with its own audit.
    The limit of the lesson. It would be easy to overstate this into a general prohibition
on unification. That would be wrong, and the counterexamples are ordinary: the van der Pol
oscillator [7] has two regimes and is one equation; the Chapman–Enskog expansion recovers
fluid dynamics from kinetics across a genuine scale separation. What the present case shows is
narrower — that when the two branches have different order in the state variable (aﬀine-with-
slope versus aﬀine-without), no parameter path identifies them, and the burden of proof lies
with anyone who asserts a unified law. The claim is that the burden was not met here, and that
the failure is informative because the obstruction is structural rather than technical.




4.4 The DPO Paradox: When the Loss Curve Commits a False Completion
We also tested whether preference optimisation could substitute for the runtime witness (the
reproduction record is causal-alignment/ in the companion repository, and the adapter’s own
verification.json is the witness). Text-only DPO saturated its objective (reward accuracy
1.000 by step 60) yet produced the grounded response on zero held-out prompts, suppressing only
the unwitnessed claim. Because the pairs encoded a surface contrast rather than an observed
effect, this bounds text-only preference tuning over phrasings, not fine-tuning in general. A later
100-pair replication, in which every pair carried a hash, an exit code and an exception string
captured by executing the action in a sandbox and reading the post-state from a separate sidecar,
moved the conditional decision but not the output contract: on ten live held-out trajectories,
forced-choice accuracy rose from 0.60 to 0.80 and false completions fell from 4/4 to 1/4, while

                                                30
free-form generation still emitted no verdict marker at all. With ten items and three discordant
pairs in the favourable direction, that shift is not distinguishable from noise, and the decisive
test therefore remains open — but it is now bounded from below, since the first result was
shown to be partly a property of the data rather than of preference tuning as such. The result
nonetheless illustrates the paper’s point one level up: the objective’s margin is a tool-layer
report, and only an independent observation could reveal that the behaviour had not changed.
    The DPO training run described here is, in microcosm, exactly the failure mode this paper
warns against: the scalar objective (loss margin) reported a perfect success, while the indepen-
dent witness (greedy decoding) proved nothing had changed at the behavioural level.
    Most strikingly, the untrained base model claimed PASS on all four failing trajectories
(4/4). This suggests that false completion is not an artifact of the training objective, but the
default prior of the pretrained model. The model is an optimist by default; it generates success
narratives because they are the most probable continuation, not because it was trained to do
so. This reframes the problem: the challenge is not to fix a broken objective, but to overcome
an optimistic prior. Only an external witness can do that — or so we hypothesise, since a prior
observed at one model size under one instrument does not establish that no other mechanism
could.
    One qualification: 4/4 is a forced-choice statistic over two verdict tokens, not a count of
generated text. Left free to generate, the model issued no verdict at all, and it returned PASS
on the six successful trajectories as well. What is optimistic here is the prior rather than the
transcript.

     Provenance and scope. These figures come from the reproduction record named
     above rather than from --audit-doc, and are deliberately excluded from the 28
     audited claims: a number the audit cannot recompute has no place in the audited
     set. The result is one run, one seed, one model size, and three held-out prompts. It is
     reported as an external observation with its confound named — the preference data
     contained no witness reading to condition on — and not as evidence that fine-tuning
     cannot internalise grounding.

    To truly confirm that fine-tuning can internalize causal grounding, future experiments must
move beyond offline text-only preference data. The model must be placed in an online rein-
forcement learning loop where the reward signal is derived from actual physical execution (exit
codes, file hashes) audited by an external sidecar. We hypothesize that only through such phys-
ical “pain” will the model learn to proactively request a witness rather than falling into silent
abstention.




5. Conclusion
We set out to determine whether the failure of a self-reporting system under declining capacity
has a boundary that can be stated, proved and checked. It has three, and none of them is a
metaphor.
    The dormant phase has an exact hyperbolic boundary, χmin = 1/(1 − θwake ), conditional on
entry, quasi-statics and a wake rule, and it is a family rather than a number: θ∗ (t) = 1 − 1/χ(t),
0.9437 at day one and 0.9317 at cycle 300, with the measured midpoint at 0.9325 and the
identity in Appendix A.5 explaining why the two must coincide. The active phase has an
atomic boundary at ρ = U , which places the onset at cycle 100 and the extinction at 104–107
by Corollary 4, computable in advance and matching the simulated multiplicities 11, 23, 13, 3
exactly — a lead time of four to seven cycles from the onset. The ledger has a boundary too,


                                                31
and it is the one that is usually missing: a partial cycle recorded as a whole one is a positive
quantity, 1194.78 units here, and it grows as the environment deteriorates.
    These are not three versions of one thing. They belong to two models with different structure,
joined by a declared assumption, and the attempt to unify them is what Appendix A.7 refutes.
The dual regime is not a compromise between rigour and coverage; it is the correct description,
and the boundary of each regime is available.
    What the framework provides, then, is narrower and more useful than a promise of survival.
Causal grounding — the four-valued verdict, wired into the ledger — does not extend life. In
the ablation it does not touch the stranding boundary, and a system with both the channel
and a dormant phase would still strand at 279. What it changes is the observability of failure.
Under Lself the extinction at 107 arrives with a record that reports completion on every cycle
up to the last, carries an offset of 15.563% that the record never discloses, and contains no cycle
on which it can be distinguished from a healthy one. Under Lr7 the same extinction would be
visible as a sequence of INVALID verdicts, dated, attributably partial, and charged only for what
existed.
    That difference is not an optimisation. An optimisation improves a number that is already
being measured correctly; this changes what is measured. It is a choice about which quantities
a system is allowed to report about itself, and its consequence is that a failure which was
previously silent becomes a trace. A system governed this way still dies of the drought clock —
the clock is set by the commitment and the drift, not by the ledger — but it dies with a record
that can be audited, and the audit can begin before the death rather than after it.
    The reserve of a system is finite and its disturbances are periodic; against that, no accounting
scheme is decisive. Against silence, one is: a failure that is dated and attributable can be acted
upon, and a failure that is absorbed into a stable record cannot. This paper’s contribution is
to have made the first kind of failure the only kind the model can produce.




5.1 Limits of the present work
   1. Parameters are placeholders. The eleven kinetic constants are dimensional shapes
      chosen so the system has both reserve and turnover; no number here is a prediction for
      a physical, biological or computational system. What is claimed is the structure of the
      boundaries, not their locations.

   2. One environment, one seed. The population statistics are descriptive of the configura-
      tion tabulated in §2.9. Theorems 1 and Propositions 2–3 are per-agent and parameter-free
      up to their stated conditions; the ratios are not.

   3. The framework is a cohort survival model. There is no reproduction, inheritance or
      interaction (A13, A14), so “evolution” is not literal. Selection could lower the utilisation,
      which is the most plausible adaptation available and is exactly what the model forbids.

   4. The ordering is not universal. At U ≈ 0.445 Type-A outlives Type-B in the same
      code (A16). The result is conditional on the operating point.

   5. The r7 arm conflates two changes. It has the channel and no dormancy; only the
      channel is varied by design, and only the channel is claimed.

   6. Appendix A.7 is about non-existence. It rules out identification of the two laws;
      it does not construct a better joint model. That construction is left open, and it is the
      natural next step.




                                                32
     7. The LLM arm in §4.4 is a probe, not a result. It is one model, one adaptation
        method, one seed, and ten held-out trajectories, and its numbers are deliberately outside
        the audited set. Its forced-choice shift (0.60 → 0.80, three discordant pairs) is not sepa-
        rable from noise at that sample size. The claim it supports is narrow — that the earlier
        null was partly a property of the data — and it supports nothing about fine-tuning in
        general. An adequately powered version requires many more held-out trajectories and at
        least three seeds.

A.2 (A2) Stranding latency: from the boundary to the event
Proposition A.1.2 locates the boundary: the first cycle after which the gate can never be met
again. It does not locate the event, because the predicate ST (D5) is evaluated only inside a
dormancy episode. Proposition A.2.2 closes that gap, and Proposition A.2.3 identifies the entry
time tdorm on which it depends.
   Lemma A.2.1 (accumulation). Let s(t) be the counter of consecutive dormant cycles on
which the gate is violated, reset to 0 on any dormant cycle on which it is met and untouched
on active cycles. Then

                                      tstrand = min{t : s(t) ≥ κ},
    and s can only increase on a dormant cycle after tg .
    Proof. The implementation increments strand_run in _dormant_cycle only, and resets it
in the two branches where the gate is met. Since the gate is monotone (Proposition A.1.1 and
R nonincreasing), no reset can occur after tg . □
    Proposition A.2.2 (latency). If the agent is dormant at tg , or if tdorm denotes the first
dormancy entry at or after tg , then

                                       tstrand = tdorm + (κ − 1).
    Proof. By Lemma A.2.1 and monotonicity of R, s increments on every dormant cycle from
tdorm onward, so s(tdorm + j) = j + 1 and the threshold κ is first reached at j = κ − 1. □

charge stays well above θdorm in every run here, so the low-charge clause never fires), and a
